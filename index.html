01 · The problem

The map says one thing. The field says another.

Rural land in India is still largely measured with chain and tape, sometimes from surveys a century old. Families have divided land, sold parts of it and moved bunds since, and the records never caught up.

01

Outdated ground truth

Chain-and-tape surveys, some from the colonial era, no longer match real boundaries after partition and inheritance.

02

Map ≠ record

Cadastral maps, Record of Rights, mutation and registration data live in separate systems and rarely agree.

03

Disputes & delay

Encroachments, unrecorded sales and overlapping claims feed long civil litigation.

Our answer
Drone speed with rover-grade legal accuracy.

Every boundary is verified by its owner on site and linked to its land record the same day.

02 · How BhuMap works

Fly, walk, verify, sync.

The drone covers the whole village in one sortie. The rover only goes where a boundary is in doubt. Owners and the revenue official sign off in the field.

1

Aerial capture

An RTK drone maps the whole village at 80–100 m, about 2–3 cm per pixel.

2

Ground truth

The RTK rover logs cm-level points at the boundaries that the drone map can't settle.

3

Verify

The owner and the revenue official confirm each boundary and sign on the tablet.

4

Sync

The parcel gets a geo-referenced ID, linked to RoR, mutation and registration data in GIS.

04 · Why BhuMap

Two layers of accuracy, one linked record.

Two-layer accuracy

The drone gives fast, full coverage. The RTK rover gives cm-level ground truth at every doubtful boundary point.

Owner-verified boundaries

The farmer and the revenue official confirm each boundary on site in the mobile app.

One linked record

Every parcel gets a geo-referenced ID tied to RoR, mutation and registration data.

Speed & cost

Drones cover villages far faster than chain crews. Rovers are sent only where a boundary needs confirming.
